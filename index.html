STAGE 00
Toolkit & Lab Safety
Linux, Git, an editor, documentation habits and safe lab practice. Every later stage assumes this works.
⚠ START HERE -- Do Not Skip

Prerequisites: None. A computer you can install software on. WSL needs Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11; otherwise use a native Linux install.

Why this stage matters: Every simulation, firmware build and project in this roadmap runs through a shell and Git. Safety habits formed now prevent the burns, shocks and dead boards that end many beginners' progress.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Run a Linux shell on your machine (WSL or native) and move around the file system confidently.
  • Create, commit, branch and push a Git repository to GitHub.
  • Write notes and READMEs in Markdown from an editor you configured.
  • Explain and apply basic electrical, battery and ESD safety rules before touching hardware.
  • Keep a project folder structure and a daily engineering log.
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. Safety first: mains and battery hazards, ESD, soldering and fume safety, fuses and current-limited supplies
  • 2. Install Linux: WSL on Windows, or dual boot, or a virtual machine; compare and choose
  • 3. Shell basics: navigation, files, permissions, pipes, searching, package install
  • 4. Git: init, add, commit, branch, merge, remote, push, pull request
  • 5. Editor setup and Markdown documentation
  • 6. Project hygiene: folder layout, naming, README, engineering log
📗

STAGE 01
Mathematics I: Algebra, Trigonometry, Calculus
The language of every later stage: functions, rates of change and accumulation.

Prerequisites: Stage 00. School arithmetic.

Why this stage matters: Rates of change and accumulation describe capacitor charging, inductor current, signal power and almost every equation in later stages. Weak calculus is the most common reason engineering students stall in circuits and signals.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Manipulate algebraic and trigonometric expressions fluently
  • Differentiate and integrate standard functions and apply them to rates and areas
  • Use limits, series and exponentials to model charging and decay
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. Algebra and equations, inequalities, logarithms and exponentials
  • 2. Functions and graphs, trigonometry, radians, identities
  • 3. Limits and continuity
  • 4. Derivatives, rules, optimisation, related rates
  • 5. Integrals, techniques, applications
  • 6. Sequences, series, Taylor series
📗

Skills Checklist

  • Rearrange equations and solve for any variable
  • Use logarithms and exponentials fluently
  • Convert degrees and radians and use trigonometric identities
  • Compute limits and recognise continuity
  • Differentiate sums, products, quotients and chains
  • Integrate by substitution and by parts
  • Find maxima and minima

  • Interpret a derivative as a rate and an integral as an area

  • Dropping the constant of integration

  • Memorising rules without checking results with a plot
  • Only reading solutions instead of solving problems

STAGE 02
Mathematics II: Differential Equations, Linear Algebra, Complex Numbers
The tools for circuits, signals and control: ODEs, matrices and phasors.

Prerequisites: Stage 01.

Why this stage matters: Circuits with capacitors and inductors, control systems and signals are all differential equations. Linear algebra solves networks and underlies simulation, and complex numbers turn AC analysis into algebra.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Solve first and second order ODEs and interpret the response
  • Solve linear systems and understand eigenvalues
  • Use complex numbers, phasors and Laplace transforms in circuit problems
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. First-order ODEs
  • 2. Second-order linear ODEs, damping and resonance
  • 3. Laplace transform and its use for ODEs
  • 4. Vectors, matrices, determinants, linear systems
  • 5. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
  • 6. Complex numbers, Euler formula, phasors
  • 7. Multivariable calculus and vector calculus basics
📗

  • Solve second-order ODEs with constant coefficients

  • Apply the Laplace transform and its inverse

STAGE 03
Probability, Statistics and Numerical Methods
Handling noise, tolerance and approximation, which all real measurements need.

Prerequisites: Stage 02.

Why this stage matters: Every real measurement has noise and every component has tolerance. Probability and numerical methods let you predict how wrong a circuit can be and compute answers when no formula exists.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Compute probabilities, expectations and variances
  • Treat measurement noise and component tolerance statistically
  • Apply root finding, interpolation and numerical integration
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. Counting and probability axioms
  • 2. Random variables, common distributions, Gaussian noise
  • 3. Expectation, variance, correlation
  • 4. Estimation, confidence intervals, regression
  • 5. Root finding and interpolation
  • 6. Numerical integration and ODE solvers
  • 7. Error and floating-point arithmetic
📗

STAGE 04
Physics I: Mechanics, Waves, Heat and Materials
The physical base under every device: energy, motion, oscillation and heat.

Prerequisites: Stage 01.

Why this stage matters: Energy, oscillation, waves and heat are the physical language behind every device. Wave behaviour comes back in transmission lines, and heat limits every power circuit you will build.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply Newton laws, energy and momentum
  • Describe oscillations and waves
  • Reason about heat flow and basic material properties
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. Units, vectors, kinematics
  • 2. Forces and Newton laws
  • 3. Work, energy, power
  • 4. Momentum and rotation
  • 5. Oscillations and waves
  • 6. Temperature and heat transfer
  • 7. Material properties: conductors, insulators, strength
📗

STAGE 06
DC Circuit Analysis
Voltage, current, resistance, power and the laws that solve any resistive network.

Prerequisites: Stages 02 and 05 (basics).

Why this stage matters: Every analysis method in electronics starts here. Ohm and Kirchhoff laws, node and mesh analysis and Thevenin equivalents are used daily by working engineers, and mistakes here carry into every later stage.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply Ohm, Kirchhoff, node and mesh analysis
  • Use Thevenin, Norton and superposition
  • Simulate and build simple DC circuits and measure them
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)

  • 1. Charge, current, voltage, power
  • 2. Resistors, series and parallel
  • 3. Kirchhoff laws
  • 4. Node and mesh analysis
  • 5. Superposition, Thevenin, Norton
  • 6. Op-amp ideal model (intro)
  • 7. Capacitors and inductors in DC
  • 8. Measurement with a multimeter
📗

STAGE 08

  • 7. Device models for simulation
  • 8. Reading datasheets

STAGE 09
Analog Electronics
Amplifiers, op-amps, feedback and filters: designing circuits with gain.

Prerequisites: Stage 08.

Why this stage matters: Gain, filtering and signal conditioning are done with analog circuits, and even digital chips depend on analog behaviour at their pins. This stage is where circuit intuition is built, and it decays quickly without practice.

🎯

Learning Outcomes

  • Bias and analyse BJT and MOSFET amplifiers
  • Design op-amp circuits and active filters
  • Understand feedback, stability and noise basics
🧭

Subject Breakdown (in order)
